# Field Notes: The Alpine Marmot

## Appearance
The Alpine marmot is a large ground squirrel that lives in the high meadows of the Alps. Adults measure about 50 centimetres from nose to rump, with a bushy tail of a further 15 centimetres. In late summer an adult can weigh up to 8 kilograms, roughly twice its weight in spring after hibernation. The fur is grey-brown, and the large front claws are well suited to digging.

## Habitat
Marmots live between 800 and 3200 metres above sea level, on sunny slopes with deep soil where they can dig burrows. A family burrow system can have dozens of entrances and tunnels up to ten metres long. They prefer open terrain with good visibility, which helps them spot predators such as golden eagles and foxes.

## Social life
Marmots live in family groups of up to twenty animals, led by a dominant breeding pair. Only the dominant female normally reproduces, giving birth to between one and seven young once a year. When a marmot spots danger it gives a loud whistle; a single short whistle warns of an eagle, while a series of whistles signals a threat on the ground.

## Hibernation
Marmots hibernate for six months, from about October to April, with the whole family sealed together in one chamber lined with dry grass. During hibernation their heart rate falls from around 100 beats per minute to as few as five, and body temperature drops to about 5 degrees Celsius. Every few weeks they briefly rewarm, and the adults huddle around the young to keep them from freezing.

## Diet
In summer marmots feed on grasses, herbs, flowers and roots, eating most heavily in the weeks before hibernation to build up fat reserves. They rarely drink, because they get most of the water they need from fresh plants and morning dew.
